## Product Updates

### Claude Solves the 25-Year-Old NIST Curve-Seed Mystery
- **Summary**: Anthropic reports an internal Claude research model traced the unexplained seeds behind NIST's ECDSA elliptic curves — supplied by the NSA in 2000 with no provenance, fueling backdoor suspicion for 25 years — to `lrand48`, a simple C RNG initialized with Unix timestamps between May 1997 and April 1998. The tell: lrand48 only outputs 31 bits, and top bits in some seeds were systematically zero. Anthropic says this makes deliberate backdoors less likely and is donating the $36,864 bounty to the Internet Security Research Group.
- **Source**: https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-claude-cracks-a-25-year-old-nsa-cryptography-mystery-in-under-30-minutes/
- **Significance**: The highest-profile "AI solves a long-standing open question" result since the nine-loop physics calculation — and one with direct security relevance, since ECDSA underpins most of the internet's signatures. Note it's a provenance answer, not a cryptographic break.

### Claude Designs Leaner Quantum Circuits Than Published Best
- **Summary**: Anthropic says Claude improved on the best published designs for eight foundational quantum-computing routines — in seven of eight tasks using both fewer qubits and fewer costly gates (in some cases 10x fewer); in the eighth (point addition, worked on for 53 hours) it found a genuinely new approach trading 12% more qubits for 17x fewer gates, with machine-checked proofs. Led by Anthropic fellow Andi Gu with Harvard's Susanne Yelin and Mikhail Lukin and QuEra Computing.
- **Source**: https://framesignal.com/stories/anthropic-claude-beats-best-quantum-computer-designs
- **Significance**: Third domain (physics, cryptography, quantum) in a month where Claude produced research-grade results. The ART attribution dispute's lesson applies: watch for provenance questions here too.

### How Anthropic Reviews Code When Claude Writes 90% of It
- **Summary**: The Claude Code team published its review model: Anthropic's monorepo went from ~2,500 changes/day in May to 30,000+ in September; Claude writes ~90% of Claude Code's own code. Low-risk changes (docs, copy, feature-flagged code) that pass AI review + security checks skip human review; everything else requires a person, and "an agent's approval never counts as a person's." Result (self-reported, June→Sept): merged PRs more than doubled while reverts fell 40%+.
- **Source**: https://secondsource.io/en/issues/2026-10-17/
- **Significance**: The first detailed public playbook for risk-tiered review at agent-scale code velocity. The revert-rate-falling claim is the part to verify independently — but the "what could break, and which check catches it" reframing is the right question for our own pipelines.
